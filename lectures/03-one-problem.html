Last week, in one slide

An LLM is our neuron’s training loop at scale: tokens become vectors; attention lets each token take what it needs from the others; the block is stacked dozens of times.

At the end, one knob per token — and temperature decides how boldly it picks.

Pre-training, fine-tuning and RL are the same loop with different right answers.

It invents by design: there is always a top knob, even when the fact was never in the data.

Part 1 Explore

The question for the morning

Is crypto risk predictable — and which part of it?

Exploration is not “make charts”. It is asking the data small questions, in an order, until you know which question is worth a model.

order small question what it decides
1 what is in the table? how to join crypto and stocks
2 how big are the moves? which scale, which units
3 how often is “impossible”? whether the bell curve is allowed
4 does yesterday tell us about today? what is worth predicting

Fat tails. Big days are rare, but eighty-five times less rare than the textbook bell curve says. A “99% VaR” built on that curve underestimates exactly the days that matter.

Two questions that sound alike

Direction

BTC went up today.

Is it more likely to go up tomorrow?

Size

BTC moved a lot today, either way.

Is it more likely to move a lot tomorrow?

Bet

Which one has memory — direction, size, both, neither?

We measure memory with the autocorrelation: the correlation of a series with itself, k days earlier. 0 means no memory.

The answer, on eight years of BTC

lag direction (return) size (absolute return)
1 day −0.05 0.17
2 days 0.04 0.13
3 days 0.01 0.11
4 days 0.03 0.17
5 days 0.02 0.14

Computed in the notebook, 7 Oct 2026. The size column stays positive for weeks.

Calm follows calm. Storms follow storms.

The direction of tomorrow has no memory. Its size does. This is called volatility clustering — and we found it before training anything.

Not a constant: BTC and the stock market

BTC–SPY correlation 0.05 0.06 0.36 0.28 0.57 0.34 0.28 0.48 0.47

“Digital gold, uncorrelated with stocks” was roughly true before 2020 and has not been true since.

A correlation is a series, not a number. It has regimes too — and a portfolio built on the 2019 number learned that in 2022.

Part 2 Predict

Three ways to write the same decision

Three words, and the whole of supervised learning

feature what you know at day t
target what happens after t
horizon how long after — today, 7 days

\text{features at } t \;\longrightarrow\; \text{model} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{target over } (t, t+7]

Two targets, the same nine features:

  • y_dir — will BTC be up over the next 7 days?
  • y_vol — will the next 7 days be more volatile than usual?

After Part 1 you can already guess how this ends. Write your guess down.

The wall

For every feature, write the latest day it uses. For the target, recompute one date by hand. One minute each, every time.

Before the model: what does nothing score?

Bet

Share of 7-day windows in which BTC went up?

target share of 1s “always say the majority”
up next week 53% 53%
high-vol next week 46% 54%

That is the base rate — the number to beat. A model that does not beat it is a cost with a dashboard.

The black box: a random forest

Hundreds of decision trees. Each is a flowchart of yes/no questions on the features — “is this week’s vol above 1.3× usual?” — grown on a slightly different sample of the past.

They vote. The share of votes is the probability.

It finds interactions and curves on its own, which a regression does not.

What it cannot do is tell you whether it is right.

That is the split’s job — and ours.

We open it in week 9. Today we only judge it from the outside — which is exactly what you will do with every vendor model.

Why the shuffled number lies

Shuffle a time series and the model trains on Wednesday and Friday, then is tested on Thursday. It is not predicting — it is remembering its neighbours.

What came out

base rate AUC, shuffled AUC, honest the rule “next week like this week”
direction 53% 0.70 0.55 —
volatility 54% 0.74 0.60 0.64

Computed in the notebook, 7 Oct 2026; your live run may differ in the second decimal. AUC: 0.5 is a coin, 1.0 is perfect.

Read it in this order

  1. Shuffled > honest, on both. The gap is leakage, not skill. On direction it turns a coin into a “signal”.
  2. Direction ≈ a coin. Hundreds of trees, nine features, nothing to say about next week’s direction — what Part 1 told us for free.
  3. Volatility is predictable — and the one-line rule beats the forest.

You only know the rule wins because you built the rule first. Most AI pitches never do.

Same data, same model. The question decides.

Whether ML has anything to offer depends on the question you ask it. Choosing the question is the manager’s job — and the exploration is how you choose.

What did the black box look at?

We cannot read 300 trees. We can ask: if I scramble one feature, how much worse does it get?

That is permutation importance, measured on the test years.

  • the volatility features dominate (vol_30, vol_7), with the size of last week’s move (ret_7) behind them — Part 1 again;
  • a black box that agrees with what you saw by eye is one you can start to trust;
  • one that leans on weekday is one you should not.
