Portfolio (60%) — your capstone

Due: Sunday 6 December 2026, 23:59 · Moodle ▸ Turnitin (the memo) and your Drive folder ese-ai-fintech/capstone/ (everything else) · presentation Wednesday 9 December, 10:00 (proposed)

The idea

Pick one real decision in finance or crypto that somebody takes every week. Build, with the tools of this course, an AI tool that helps take it: one that reads documents, looks at data, or acts within rules you wrote. Write the test before you build. Run it, put it next to a rival that does no work, price it, and say what you would not let it do. Then defend it for fifteen minutes.

It is the midterm again, with one difference: this time you choose the question, and the tool is yours.

No code. Everything is done with the tools you have used since September: Gemini Notebook, ChatGPT or Claude, a sheet, an agent you configure.

Three kinds of capstone

Choose one. The examples are only examples.

The tool… built with for example
reads (session 7) a notebook or a saved prompt over documents A stablecoin claim checker: does what an issuer says match its own reserve report, with the page for each answer?
looks (session 8) an assistant over data you exported A stablecoin peg watcher: five past depegs, what the data showed before each one, what the assistant flags and what it misses
acts (session 9) the rules of an agent, tested case by case A treasury agent that pays supplier invoices in USDT: what it may do alone, what needs a person, tested on ten cases including traps

Your MCP case from homework 5 can be your capstone if it fits one of the three.

Four questions every idea must pass

Bring three ideas to session 8 (Wednesday 25 November), each answering these four. We keep one.

  1. Can you open the source today? The documents or the data, in your hands, not promised.
  2. Can you write ten questions or cases with the answers found by hand? If you cannot, you cannot test it.
  3. Does it fit in two weeks? Smaller is better. A narrow tool, well tested, beats a broad one nobody checked.
  4. Is it still interesting if the answer is “no, it does not work”? If a failed test makes the project worthless, it was a bet, not a question.

4. The tool Mon 30 Nov Build it. Write down the model, its context window, its knowledge cutoff and its prices, from the model card.
5. The draft Tue 1 Dec The tool, the test run once, the memo’s headings with three lines under each. Reviewed in session 10.
6. Run, price, write Sun 6 Dec Run the test on the final version, name every miss, price it, write the memo, rehearse.

What to submit

On Moodle, to the Turnitin submission for the portfolio, by Sunday 6 December, 23:59: the memo, PDF, about 2,000 words (1,800–2,300), with your matriculation number and not your name. Under these headings:

  • Decision and user: who decides what, how often, and what a wrong decision costs today.
  • What the tool does: five sentences and one diagram. The model card: which model, context window, knowledge cutoff, prices.
  • The test and the honest result: the table, the score next to the rival’s, every miss named (retrieval, generation, memory, or rule broken).
  • Cost: per question or per case, with tokens in, tokens out and both prices visible. A month at the volume your user needs, and the time saved against a person doing it by hand.
  • Risks and controls: a table with four columns: risk, control, law or request, who signs. Then MiCA and the EU AI Act where they apply, one paragraph each.
  • What would have to be true: for the user to rely on it, and the first thing that would make you switch it off.
  • AI-use disclosure: see below.

In your Drive folder ese-ai-fintech/capstone/, shared with the tutor:

  1. The tool: the link to the notebook with its sources, or the ChatGPT or Claude project (its instructions and the model), or the agent’s rules file. Someone else must be able to open it and run your test.
  2. The test sheet: ten questions or cases written first, the answer found by hand with its source, the tool’s answer, hit or miss, which kind of miss, the rival’s answer. Plus the three traps.
  3. The cost: the token counts you read, the prices you used, the arithmetic.

The presentation

Fifteen minutes on Wednesday 9 December, from your laptop, with the tool live. Six beats:

Beat Minutes The question it answers
1. The decision 2 Who decides what, and what does a mistake cost?
2. The tool 3 What does it do? Show it, on one real case.
3. The test 3 How many of ten, against the rival? Say the number.
4. The cost 2 What does one answer cost, and one month?
5. What could go wrong 3 The worst miss, the trap that worked, the control that stops it.
6. What would have to be true 2 For someone to use it on Monday morning.

Then questions. I will pick lines of your memo and cells of your sheet at random and ask you to explain them.

How it is marked

The five criteria of the syllabus, the same as the midterm.

Weight Criterion What earns it
15% Problem framing and business relevance A real decision, a named user, a cost of being wrong.
25% Data, evidence and AI methods The test written first, answers found by hand, a rival, every miss named.
20% The artefact A tool that someone else can open and run, and that does what the memo says.
20% Risk, ethics, governance and oversight Controls that are laws, not requests; who signs; MiCA and the AI Act where they apply; the disclosure.
20% Clarity, communication and reflection A memo within length, a presentation within time, an honest account of what did not work.

A point counts when the sheet, a number or the tool shows it, not when a paragraph asserts it.

AI-use disclosure

Assistants are allowed and expected. Say which ones you used and for what (finding sources, drafting, checking, the tool itself), which parts you did without them, three things an assistant got wrong that you caught (where, and how you caught it), and one sentence confirming that nothing confidential from any employer went into an AI tool. “I used ChatGPT for help” is not a disclosure.

Rules

  • No real money. An agent that acts is tested on cases written in a sheet, or on a test network. Nothing in the course is investment advice.
  • Nothing confidential goes into an AI tool.
  • No new source in the last three days unless the current one is broken. Polish beats scope.
  • Every line you submit, you can explain.
